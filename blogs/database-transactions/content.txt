Where Did the Money Go?

Imagine transferring ₹10,000 to Sony.

Your account is debited. Hers isn't credited.

Where did the money go?

That's exactly the kind of nightmare database transactions are designed to prevent.

A transaction groups multiple database operations into a single all-or-nothing unit. When both debit and credit belong to one database transaction:

• Debit ₹10,000 from Account A.
• Credit ₹10,000 to Account B.
• COMMIT only if both succeed.
• If either fails, ROLLBACK both changes.

This is atomicity, the A in ACID. Either the entire transfer succeeds, or none of it does.

One twist: a network timeout doesn't necessarily mean the transaction failed. The server may have committed even if the response never reached the client. That is why retry safety and idempotency matter too.

And what happens when the money moves across different databases or services? That's a bigger distributed-systems challenge—and a perfect topic for the next post on Two-Phase Commit (2PC).
