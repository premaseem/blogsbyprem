LINKEDIN CAPTION
🚗 Your car gives 12 MPG. The dealer promised 15. Your goal was 20.

That’s a great way to understand SLI, SLO, and SLA.

These terms are often explained in SRE, but they go beyond uptime. They are useful across data, software, and AI platforms when you need to define a target, measure performance, and make a promise.

🎯 SLO (Service Level Objective): Your target. “I want my car to give 20+ MPG.”
📊 SLI (Service Level Indicator): Your measurement. “Average mileage this month is 18 MPG.”
🤝 SLA (Service Level Agreement): Your contractual promise. “This car will deliver at least 15 MPG under agreed conditions.”

SLO = what you want.
SLI = what you measure.
SLA = what you promise, including consequences if the agreement is breached.

Now apply it to a data pipeline:
• SLO: 95% of events should arrive within 5 minutes.
• SLI: measured data-arrival delay and percentage meeting the five-minute window.
• SLA: downstream customers are promised delivery within 15 minutes, subject to agreed remedies.

Or an AI platform:
• SLO: target response latency, knowledge freshness, or answer quality.
• SLI: measured latency, retrieval success, or evaluated answer quality.
• SLA: the formal service commitment to users.

A metric alone is just a number. An objective gives it meaning. An agreement gives it accountability.

💬 Do your teams distinguish SLI, SLO, and SLA clearly?
